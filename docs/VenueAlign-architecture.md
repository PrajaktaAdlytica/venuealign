# VenueAlign • website architecture and editorial direction

## The buying decision

A touring production manager has a signed rider and a venue inventory. The manager needs to know which local substitutions a technical lead has reviewed and which exceptions still need a call. The first commercial conversation funds a **Venue Fit** comparison against one season of past advances. The second step needs a data owner, a named reviewer for each stop, a baseline and a signed scope. Keeping the walkthrough and pilot separate lets someone inspect that decision before submitting any information.

The November concept brief supplies the working hypotheses. VenueAlign is the requested public identity. StageRigfolio and stagerigfolio.com belong to the earlier portfolio draft. The November 24 registry note and pending clearance concerned that earlier name. Ownership and trademark clearance for VenueAlign are unverified. The $100,000 figure is the user's platform ambition. The offer uses the brief's $18,000 annual price hypothesis and a separately scoped pilot.

## Content placement

The homepage carries the buyer narrative. The product pages explain actual inputs and handoffs. The demo records the decision. The pilot page carries the quote, prerequisites, protocol and decision gate. The research and validation pages cover acquisition, tests, internal commercial assumptions and the later tracking model. This keeps an ARR scenario or CRM field list available in context.

## Concept-to-business handoff

**Inputs.** Approved rider, venue plan, approved inventory, crew availability, final technical decisions and rental amendments. Name the operator, rights holder, reviewer for each stop, contracting buyer and renewal owner.

**Comparison.** Start with a past case and a signed protocol. Use a season as the sample. Agree inclusion criteria, reviewer, timers, source-quality rules, adverse outcomes and thresholds before measuring the two processes.

**Decision.** Review the score with the buyer. Funded scope, authorized representative data and reviewer agreement are the gate for moving beyond the isolated preview. Evaluate recurring use and profile reuse only after that gate.

**Acquisition.** A LinkedIn teardown can show the rider passage, dated inventory, documented substitute and open question. Test the tagline against an annotated exception-packet message with touring managers, suppliers and venue networks. Count qualified meetings, permission and paid interest. Track consent and spend separately.

**Portfolio context.** The earlier StageRigfolio name and its proposed domain are provenance. A50 and Ramp Harbor are portfolio references. Ramp Harbor concerns aircraft turnaround; VenueAlign concerns live-event equipment and venue decisions. These comparisons belong in the builder record.

**Commercial arithmetic.** 56 equivalent subscriptions at $18,000 represent $1,008,000 ARR. Buyer count, willingness to pay, renewal and delivery cost still need evidence. The $100,000 platform ambition is distinct from the buyer-facing offer.

**Shared acceptance evidence.** Four product pages, the five-show case, two proposed substitutes, one open spacing item, source/version fields, local reviewer action, fictional export, buyer account, evaluation CSV, capture widths, event vocabulary and consent choice are mapped to URLs in the website. The copy deck lists the actual page text in reading order.
