What do we need for a pilot?

## Why change the advance?

[Open page](../public/problem.html)

Why change

### A familiar rider. An unfamiliar room.

A fixture appears on a signed rider. The local inventory lists a different unit. The plan and staffing sheet have their own owners and revision dates.

Inspect Venue Fit

#### Where the work breaks apart

**The desk**

The coordinator compares a signed rider and an advance checklist with the latest house files. The important detail can be a changed revision.

**The room**

A drawn dimension may use a different reference from the rider. The local lead needs to confirm the measurement and setup.

**The handoff**

A last-minute rental amendment can settle the show without preserving who made the decision or which alternative they reviewed.

Product workflow

#### Turn a signed rider into a list the crew can check.

Working ownerAdvance coordinator

Approved inputsSigned tour rider and its revision history

HandoffA reviewable list of requirements

**Read the signed version**

Identify the current rider, revision date and tour contact. Keep the approved original available to the reviewer.

**Extract the requests**

The proposed document model identifies fixture types, dimensions, staff calls and timing. Each extracted line carries a passage and page reference.

**Resolve ambiguous wording**

Send an unclear requirement back to the production manager. A missing unit or quantity stays visible when the requirement moves into Venue Fit.

#### Before any real records enter a pilot

**Permission**

The data owner authorizes representative rider files, venue drawings and rental inventories. Record the source rights and versions in the signed scope.

Crew expertise, inventory currency and local verification determine whether a suggestion is useful. Qualified staff review power and rigging assumptions.
