Visual territories

Three ways into the same decision.

The Advance Desk

An engraved profile fixture sits between two dated source excerpts. A burgundy rule leads into the review ledger.

Visual language
Bone paper, oxblood, warm ink. A tall editorial serif names the decision. Small sans-serif labels carry document ID, date and reviewer. The object library uses front elevations and orthographic line drawings.
Scroll story
The rider passage enters from the left. The inventory arrives from the right. Witness lines connect the conflicting fields. The ruler becomes the divider in the live record.
Design cost
Requires four consistent original technical illustrations. Keep fine engraving outside the dense workbench.

House Lights

An amber-lit auditorium establishes the scale of the show. The view settles on one practical decision at the front-of-house desk.

Visual language
Velvet black, amber light, ivory paper. Newsreader for the stage story, Manrope for controls. Use photographic fields for location and flat strips for crew cues.
Scroll story
Bring the image up from a single dark tone. Reveal the headline by lines. At the next chapter the edge of the stage becomes the five-stop rail. The camera stops moving when evidence becomes readable.
Design cost
The photographs must leave dark copy space on mobile. This opening is closest to the current homepage and needs the strongest transition into the product.

Tour Atlas

The five-show route is the hero. Selecting Meridian Hall turns one map stop into its corresponding exception queue.

Visual language
Cream cartographic paper, ink-blue type, cobalt route. A five-node schematic, a stage plan, a cue timeline and a packet index form the illustration family. The hero map is fictional; no real venue coordinates are implied.
Scroll story
Trace five stops in sequence. Hold on the third. Carry the stop label into a dark itinerary band, then align it with the dashboard heading. Visitors can jump directly to any labeled chapter.
Design cost
Small screens need a simplified route diagram and a text itinerary. Fine terrain belongs behind the map, never behind the comparison table.

Production copy for all three studies. Use the existing VenueAlign vector mark and Night Index as the tour name. In the Tour Atlas table, the two fixture alternatives belong to VF-031. The VF-032 evidence reads “8.0 m clear width requested. 7.4 m between masking lines. Field measurement pending.” Its state is Open.

Graphic system

One rule for every moving part.

ElementProduction direction
CompositionKeep every card, plan, table, and text block square to the viewport. Depth comes from light, occlusion, foreground scale and layer order. Use photographic perspective only within a photograph.
Type and spacingSet hero type at 80 to 112 px on large screens and 48 to 64 px on phones. Set interface text at 16 px; reserve monospace for IDs, revisions and dimensions. Use 8 px spacing increments, 24 px card padding and a 12-column desktop grid.
Evidence marksSolid border for a documented source, dotted border plus “Evidence incomplete” for missing support, signed row plus reviewer name for an approval. Always add state text.
Maps and illustrationsTour map shows the sequence of five imaginary stops. Stage diagram shows two labeled measurement references. Draw modules as a source page, a front-view fixture, a time axis and a flat packet index.
Directional movementCopy rises on arrival. Inputs enter horizontally from the side where they will stay. An annotation drops from the field it explains. A decision history row unfolds downward. Use one dominant movement per chapter.
Lenis and GSAPUse Lenis for wheel smoothing on motion-enabled desktop marketing pages. Drive lenis.raf(time * 1000) from a single GSAP ticker. Send Lenis scroll events to ScrollTrigger.update and refresh after fonts and layout settle. Remove listeners and timelines on route exit.
Desktop timelineUse gsap.matchMedia for breakpoint and reduced-motion branches. Trigger normal sections at about top 82%. Aim for 450 to 650 ms entrances and 60 to 90 ms stagger. Reserve scroll scrubbing for the map trace and the four-product shelf.
Controls and focusPin at most one narrative object per chapter. Include a skip-to-next-chapter link on the shelf and retain direct product links throughout. Escape closes menus and dialogs. Hover indicates focusable actions with a rule or tint; keep controls upright.
Touch and reduced motionAt narrow widths use a vertical chapter sequence and a swipeable product rail. Retain native scrolling in forms, dialogs and the workbench. With reduced motion enabled, render final states with visible labels and zero parallax, pinning, or delayed text.
PerformanceOnly the current chapter runs a timeline. Keep the hero poster immediately available. Lazy-load later photography and stop idle motion when the document is hidden. Test the wheel, touch, keyboard, reverse scroll and direct links at 390 and 1440 px.
